A sinking fund is a pot set aside for an expense that is known but not
monthly. MoneyHelper describes it as money paid into regularly for an
expense that is coming: an annual insurance bill, the car's MOT, school
uniforms, holidays or celebrations. Spreading the cost keeps the month the
bill lands from becoming a crunch.

The arithmetic is the total cost divided by the months left. A £480
insurance renewal due in eight months needs £60 set aside each month, so
the envelope holds the full amount by the time the bill arrives. Anything
already in the envelope reduces the monthly figure by its share.

Sinking funds differ from an emergency fund in what they pay for: the
sinking fund covers costs that are known, and the emergency fund covers
costs that are not. MoneyHelper notes that a fund built from scratch starts
with the emergency pot first, because a broken boiler cannot be scheduled.
