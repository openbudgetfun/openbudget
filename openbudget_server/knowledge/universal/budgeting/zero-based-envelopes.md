Zero-based budgeting means income minus assigned expenses equals zero at the
start of the month. In OpenBudget it happens with envelopes: each envelope
holds money for one kind of spending, and assigning continues until no
income is left unassigned. The assignment is a plan, not a payment — money
moves between envelopes whenever the plan changes.

A standing start follows three steps. First, list the income the month will
bring. Second, fill envelopes for the costs the month will actually meet,
starting with obligations such as rent, utilities and food, then the
irregular costs, then goals and wants. Third, check the arithmetic: the
envelopes together hold exactly what the income provides, so an overfilled
plan shows up as a shortfall now rather than as debt later.

The US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau describes the same method
without envelopes: record income, record spending, and build a budget from
the two until income minus spending is a deliberate number, then update it
when income or habits change.
