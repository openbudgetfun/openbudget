AER — the annual equivalent rate — is what a savings balance earns over a
full year with compounding included, shown as a percentage. MoneyHelper
describes it as the total interest received on savings in one year, and the
figure to read when comparing accounts, because it puts monthly and yearly
payouts on one scale.

Compounding is why two accounts with the same headline rate can pay
differently. A rate paid monthly earns interest on earlier interest, so its
AER lands above its gross rate; a rate paid once a year has an AER equal to
the gross rate. The US label for the same idea is APY, the annual
percentage yield.

AER says nothing about fees, access rules or how long a rate lasts, and tax
may still apply to the interest. MoneyHelper's interest guide shows the
arithmetic: £1,000 at 2% for one year earns £20, giving £1,020.
