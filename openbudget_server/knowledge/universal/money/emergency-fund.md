An emergency fund is money set aside for costs that cannot be planned: a
repair, a broken appliance, a drop in income. MoneyHelper's rule of thumb is
to hold three to six months of essential outgoings — rent or mortgage, food,
heating and other bills that cannot be paused — in an account the money can
leave quickly.

The arithmetic uses the household's own figures. Essential outgoings of
£1,400 a month point to a target between £4,200 and £8,400. Any amount
helps: MoneyHelper suggests starting smaller and saving regularly, because a
small fund that exists serves better than a large one that never starts.

Order matters too. MoneyHelper warns that expensive debts — credit cards,
payday loans, mortgage arrears — usually cost more than savings earn, so
they are generally cleared before the fund is filled. Insurance is the
other route to covering unexpected costs, and it can sit alongside the
fund.
