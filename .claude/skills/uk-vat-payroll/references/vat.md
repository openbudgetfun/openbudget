Research date: 5 November 2026. Tags: **P** primary (official page or table read), **P-snip** (official page seen only through a search or fetch summary; re-read before relying), **S** secondary (accountancy-firm or other non-official page), **I** inferred.

| Payments on account         | quarterly filers owing over 2,300,000 in any 12 months or less: interim payments at the end of months 2 and 3, each annual liability / 24; balance with the return; removed 6 months after liability falls below 2,300,000 | P   |

- Thresholds are the same for April 2025 and April 2026 (Budget 2025 annex). A "frozen to 2030" claim was not confirmed.
- Due date logic (I): end of the month after period end, plus 7 days. A period ending 30 September 2026 is due 7 November 2026 (a Saturday; the deadline stands). A period ending 31 October is due 7 December. Direct Debit needs a few days' margin.
- Payments on account are irrelevant for small businesses (owing 2,300,000 implies turnover above the cash and annual accounting limits). Warn at most when trailing 12-month liability approaches it (I).
- Registration mechanics (30-day backward look, 30-day forward look, effective dates), voluntary registration and deregistration procedure were **not retrieved**. Read Notice 700/1 before building any "you must register" alert. The existing pack's VAT watch compares tax-year turnover with 90,000, which is an approximation of the rolling 12-month test.

## 2. Rates and input tax recovery

- Standard 20%, reduced 5%, zero 0% are taxable, so related input tax is generally recoverable. Exempt supplies charge no VAT and generally block recovery (partial exemption). Outside scope: supplies by a non-taxable person, made outside the UK and Isle of Man, or not in the course of business; some outside-scope supplies still allow recovery (for example services to overseas business customers). **P**: [VAT Notice 700](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/vat-notice-700-the-vat-guide/vat-notice-700-the-vat-guide) paras 3.2 to 3.6, 10.3. The 20% and 5% figures are long-standing; Notice 700 as retrieved did not print them and no 2025/26 or 2026/27 change was found (I).
- Input tax cannot generally be reclaimed on non-business goods or services, cars, business entertainment, or costs linked to exempt supplies. A valid VAT invoice is needed ([Notice 700/12](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-to-fill-in-and-submit-your-vat-return-vat-notice-70012)).
- Partial exemption: a business with exempt and taxable supplies needs a method. De minimis figures (commonly 625 a month average and no more than 50% of input tax) were **not retrieved**; read Notice 706.
- Model (I): supply treatment enum `standard | reduced | zero | exempt | outsideScope`; purchase line `recoverable: full | blocked | partial` with `businessUsePercent`, `blockedReason`, `inputTaxClaimed`, `badDebtClockStart`. Business-use apportionment applies to **input** tax only, not to output VAT on sales.

## 3. The nine boxes (P: Notice 700/12 unless stated)

[VAT Notice 700/12](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-to-fill-in-and-submit-your-vat-return-vat-notice-70012). Boxes 1 to 5 are VAT amounts; boxes 6 to 9 are net values excluding VAT.

| Box | Content                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | VAT due on sales and other outputs: output VAT on all goods and services supplied; fuel scale charges, sales of assets, goods taken for private use, reverse-charge VAT, gifts of goods over 50 (cost, ex VAT), commission; VAT due on imports under postponed VAT accounting (PVA). Deduct VAT on credit notes issued. Excludes zero-rated exports.                                                |
| 2   | VAT due on acquisitions of goods into **Northern Ireland** from EU member states (tax point: earlier of invoice date and the 15th of the month after dispatch). The same amount is reclaimable in box 4. Zero for GB businesses.                                                                                                                                                                    |
| 3   | Total VAT due = box 1 + box 2.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| 4   | VAT reclaimed on purchases and other inputs: deductible input VAT, reverse-charge VAT, NI acquisition VAT (must equal box 2), import VAT with a valid certificate, bad debt relief, PVA VAT reclaimed in the period. Excludes goods bought wholly for personal use, business entertainment, second-hand margin-scheme purchases. Deduct VAT on credit notes received. Subject to partial exemption. |
| 5   | Net VAT = box 3 minus box 4. Negative means HMRC repays (paper form: no minus sign). Under the Annual Accounting Scheme instalments are not deducted in box 5.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| 6   | Total value of sales and other outputs, ex VAT: zero-rated, reduced, exempt, outside-scope supplies, fuel scale charge value, exports, reverse-charge transactions, deposits invoiced. Excludes personal money in, loans, dividends, gifts of money, insurance claims.                                                                                                                              |
| 7   | Total value of purchases and other inputs, ex VAT: imports, NI acquisitions (also box 9), reverse-charge transactions. Excludes wages and salaries, PAYE and NIC, drawings, loans, dividends, MOT certificates, vehicle excise duty, local authority rates, anything outside the scope of VAT.                                                                                                      |
| 8   | Supplies of goods and related costs to EU member states, ex VAT (**NI only**; also in box 6).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| 9   | Acquisitions of goods and related costs from EU member states, ex VAT (**NI only**; also in box 7).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

- **Services bought from abroad (reverse charge) and gold:** supplier box 6; customer boxes 1, 4, 6 and 7.
- **Domestic reverse charge** (mobile phones, computer chips, wholesale gas and electricity, emission allowances, wholesale telecoms, renewable energy certificates, building and construction): supplier box 6 only; customer boxes 1, 4 and 7, **not box 6**.
- **Construction domestic reverse charge:** the supplier does not charge VAT; the customer accounts in box 1 and, subject to recovery rules, reclaims in box 4. Applies when payments are reported within CIS, the supply is standard or reduced rated, and no end-user or intermediary exclusion applies; end users tell the supplier in writing. [GOV.UK guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-domestic-reverse-charge-for-building-and-construction-services), [VATREVCON31000](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-reverse-charge-for-building-and-construction-services-manual/vatrevcon31000), [VATREVCON33100](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-reverse-charge-for-building-and-construction-services-manual/vatrevcon33100).
- **Postponed import VAT (PVA):** the same import VAT appears in box 1 and box 4 (net nil for a fully recovering business). The fetched text did not say whether box 7 uses customs value or value for VAT; the worked example uses the net import value (I).
- **Rounding:** API fields suggest boxes 6 to 9 are whole numbers and boxes 1 to 5 take up to 2 decimals. Notice 700/12 as retrieved did not state rounding rules; treat the pence versus whole-pound split as an API constraint (I).
- Unconfirmed: whether box 7 includes the net value of blocked-VAT purchases (the exclusion list suggests yes); whether bad debt relief value reaches box 7.

## 4. Schemes

Cash accounting and annual accounting: join at 1,350,000 or less taxable turnover, must leave above 1,600,000. Flat Rate Scheme (FRS): join at 150,000 or less (ex VAT), must leave above 230,000. **P**: [How VAT works: thresholds](https://www.gov.uk/how-vat-works/vat-thresholds).

### Cash accounting

Pay VAT on sales when customers pay; reclaim on purchases when paid. Boxes 1, 4, 6, 7 follow payments received and made. Acquisitions and imports cannot use the scheme for box 4. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/vat-cash-accounting-scheme), [Notice 700/12](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-to-fill-in-and-submit-your-vat-return-vat-notice-70012). Test: invoice dated 20 September, paid 5 October falls in the next quarter and contributes nothing to box 1 or box 6 this period.

### Annual accounting

One return a year with advance payments of 10% of the estimated bill (monthly) or 25% (quarterly). Box 5 is the full-year liability; the year-end balance is box 5 minus instalments. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/vat-annual-accounting-scheme).

### Flat Rate Scheme ([Notice 733](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/vat-notice-733-flat-rate-scheme-for-small-businesses/vat-notice-733-flat-rate-scheme-for-small-businesses), read via summary: P-snip)

- Eligibility: expected taxable turnover (ex VAT) of 150,000 or less next year; not associated with another business, not in a VAT group, not using margin schemes; not left the scheme in the previous 12 months; no VAT-related penalty or conviction in that time.
- Flat-rate turnover = VAT-**inclusive** total of all supplies (standard, zero, reduced, exempt). Excludes private income, bank interest, non-business income, and sales of capital items where input tax was claimed. Tax = flat percentage x VAT-inclusive turnover. Customers still pay 20% VAT on invoices.
- Box 1 = flat-rate VAT plus VAT accounted for outside the scheme (for example PVA). Box 4 = qualifying capital items only. Box 6 = VAT-inclusive flat-rate turnover (per the summary of Notice 733).
- **1% first-year reduction:** first 12 months from the VAT **registration** date (not scheme join date); not available if registration was more than 12 months late.
- **Limited cost trader: 16.5%.** Relevant goods spend (including VAT) under 2% of VAT-inclusive turnover, or over 2% but under 1,000 a year (250 a quarter). Relevant goods exclude vehicle costs, food and drink, capital items and services. Also confirmed by [Alto Accounting](https://www.alto-accounting.com/insights/flat-rate-vat-scheme-guide-uk) (S).
- Capital items: a single purchase of 2,000 or more including VAT is reclaimed in box 4 outside the flat rate; on sale charge VAT at the standard rate.
- Must leave if income exceeds 230,000 in the year to the anniversary or is expected to exceed it in the next 30 days; may stay if HMRC agrees in writing that the next 12 months will not exceed 191,500. Cannot rejoin for 12 months after leaving.
- **Sector percentages (P-snip; condensed by a summarising fetch, some groupings looked unreliable; re-read [the GOV.UK table](https://www.gov.uk/vat-flat-rate-scheme/how-much-you-pay) row by row and store as versioned data):** accountancy and bookkeeping, architects/engineers/surveyors, computer and IT consultancy, lawyers 14.5; management consultancy 14; financial services 13.5; film/radio/TV, hairdressing and beauty, secretarial 13; catering 12.5; entertainment and journalism 12.5; any other activity 12; labour-only building 14.5; general building 9.5; advertising, agricultural services, photography, publishing, social work, veterinary 11; computer repair 10.5; pubs 6.5; post offices 5; retailing food, tobacco, newspapers and children's clothing 4; retailing not listed 7.5. Further sectors (wholesaling, hiring, manufacturing and others) were not trusted; do not use them.
- Under FRS, what goes in box 7 and the PVA treatment from 1 June 2022 are mentioned in Notice 700/12 but not detailed in the extract. Retail schemes were not covered.

## 5. Bad debt relief, blocked input tax, fuel, cars

- Blocked input tax: non-business goods and services, cars, business entertainment, costs linked to exempt supplies (Notice 700 para 10.3, P).
- Bad debt relief: available when a customer has not paid for taxable supplies (para 4.3, P); it is a box 4 item. The timing rule for the supplier side (relief after 6 months from the later of payment due date and supply date, and the debt written off in the accounts) was **not explicit** in the extract; verify against Notice 700/18.
- Supplier-side clawback: if input tax was reclaimed and the supplier is unpaid after 6 months from the supply date or payment due date, repay it (para 10.6.1, P).
- Fuel for private use: scale charges (Notice 700/64); VAT in box 1, value in box 6. The scale charge table, car rules (50% leasing restriction, car purchase, Notice 700/57) were **not retrieved**.
- Data model (I): flag unpaid supplier invoices at 6 months and unpaid customer invoices as bad debt relief candidates.

## 6. Making Tax Digital for VAT

- Since April 2022 all VAT-registered businesses must keep digital records and file through MTD-compatible software that keeps records, prepares the return and talks to HMRC's API. [VAT Notice 700/22](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/vat-notice-70022-making-tax-digital-for-vat/vat-notice-70022-making-tax-digital-for-vat) (P).
- **The HMRC online VAT return form closed on 1 November 2022.** Except where exempt, a user cannot key a return into the HMRC portal. HMRC signs businesses up automatically. Exempt: not reasonable or practical to use digital tools (age, health, disability, location, religious objection, no internet), insolvency procedure, already exempt from online filing, and (from 29 October 2024) the final return after VAT registration is cancelled. [News](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/less-than-one-month-left-for-vat-businesses-to-be-ready-for-making-tax-digital-filing), [exemption guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/apply-for-an-exemption-from-making-tax-digital-for-vat) (P-snip).
- **Limitation for a prepare-only app (state it to the user):** OpenBudget prepares the nine box values and a checklist; it does not submit. The user cannot submit in the HMRC portal, so they need MTD-compatible software or bridging software to send the return. A prepare-only app must therefore either become MTD software (HMRC API, Government Gateway OAuth, a "finalise" declaration, fraud-prevention headers) or produce a return or CSV that the user loads into third-party bridging software. Digital links must be kept from the app's records to the bridging tool: XML or CSV import/export, file upload and download, automated transfer, API transfer, linked spreadsheet cells are accepted; manual retyping and copy and paste are not (Notice 700/22). Manual keying into the old portal is for exempt users only.
- Required digital records: designatory data (business name, principal place of business, VAT number, schemes used); per supply: tax point, net value, VAT rate (output split by standard, reduced, zero, exempt, outside scope); per purchase: tax point, value, input tax claimed; summary data: output tax, acquisition tax, reverse-charge tax, input tax, corrections and adjustments (one line per type). Retail schemes need daily gross takings; flat rate and margin scheme users have reduced requirements.
- Bridging software connects records (including spreadsheets) to HMRC through the MTD APIs. The "soft landing" on digital links ended on 1 April 2021.
- **HMRC VAT (MTD) API v1.0** (page updated 5 August 2026; sandbox `https://test-api.service.hmrc.gov.uk`, production `https://api.service.hmrc.gov.uk`; sandbox scenarios via `Gov-Test-Scenario`): obligations, submit and view a return, liabilities and payments, penalties, customer information. Fraud-prevention header data is required by law; the header list was not retrieved. Submit: `POST /organisations/vat/{vrn}/returns`, scope `write:vat`, `Accept: application/vnd.hmrc.1.0+json`, JSON body; `finalised` must be a JSON boolean; duplicates return `DUPLICATE_SUBMISSION`; `periodKey` is a 4-character string from the obligations call. [Developer Hub](https://developer.service.hmrc.gov.uk/api-documentation/docs/api/service/vat-api/1.0) (P-snip; the OpenAPI page is JavaScript-rendered and was not read), [issue 391](https://github.com/hmrc/vat-api/issues/391) (S).
- Body fields (S; confirm against the OpenAPI): `periodKey`, `vatDueSales` (box 1), `vatDueAcquisitions` (2), `totalVatDue` (3), `vatReclaimedCurrPeriod` (4), `netVatDue` (5), `totalValueSalesExVAT` (6), `totalValuePurchasesExVAT` (7), `totalValueGoodsSuppliedExVAT` (8), `totalAcquisitionsExVAT` (9), `finalised`. Boxes 1 to 5 up to 2 decimals; box 8 a whole number. How a repayment is signed and whether `netVatDue` must be non-negative were not retrieved: keep the sign internally and check the schema.
- Open: HMRC production credential approval; whether a developer can use own-use credentials for one user without full recognition.
- Do not design fraud-prevention headers without the privacy review the repo's rules require: they carry device and user-agent data.

## 7. VAT penalties and interest (accounting periods from 1 January 2023)

[How late payment penalties work](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-late-payment-penalties-work-if-you-pay-vat-late), [penalty points and penalties](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/penalty-points-and-penalties-if-you-submit-your-vat-return-late), [remove penalty points](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/remove-penalty-points-youve-received-after-submitting-your-vat-return-late).

- **Late submission points:** one per late return. Thresholds: annual 2, quarterly 4, monthly 5; non-standard periods over 20 weeks 2, over 8 up to 20 weeks 4, up to 8 weeks 5. At the threshold a 200 penalty, and 200 for each later late return while at the threshold. Nil and repayment returns count. Exempt: first return after registration, final return after cancellation, one-off returns for odd periods. Points expire after 24 months below the threshold; at the threshold they clear only after a compliance period and filing all outstanding returns for the previous 24 months. The 24-month and compliance-period detail was seen only in search summaries; the compliance period lengths (believed 6, 12 or 24 months) are unconfirmed (P-snip).
- **Late payment:** days 1 to 15 no penalty (interest from day 1). Days 16 to 30: first penalty 3% of VAT outstanding at day 15, no second penalty. Day 31 onward: 3% at day 15 plus 3% of the balance at day 30, plus a second penalty at 10% a year on the balance, accruing daily. Excludes payments on account and Annual Accounting instalments. A time-to-pay plan can reduce or avoid penalties. **P**.
- **Official worked example:** 15,000 paid 51 days late = 450 + 450 + (15,000 x 10% x 21 / 365 = 86.30) = **986.30**.
- Effective date of the 3% and 10% rates is unresolved: the page says periods from 1 January 2023; accountancy blogs say 1 April 2025 ([Sleek](https://sleek.com/uk/resources/vat-late-payment-penalty/), [Alto](https://www.alto-accounting.com/insights/vat-late-payment-penalty), S). An older HMRC news release gives 4% a year for the second penalty; the current guidance page (3% and 10%) is treated as authoritative.
- Interest: late payment Bank Rate + 4% from 6 April 2025 (7.75% from 9 January 2026; see the dated table in `uk-personal-tax`). Store penalty and interest rates as dated configuration; refresh the interest rate before use.
